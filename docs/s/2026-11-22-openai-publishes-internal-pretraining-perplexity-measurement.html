OpenAI's chief scientist proposes perplexity as a shared yardstick for a possible speed limit on AI progress, and publishes OpenAI's own numbers

Jakub Pachocki argues labs should report pretraining perplexity so model progress can be compared and shared safety limits can be set.

Jakub Pachocki, Chief Scientist at OpenAI, argues for establishing perplexity and cross-entropy loss as a metric to compare model developers and inform shared safety standards. OpenAI is providing transparency into its pretraining progress by sharing internal perplexity measurements. Pachocki explains that AI development relies on training a compressor on large datasets. Tracking perplexity helps measure progress on the intelligence substrate. The company outlines how models from GPT-2 to losses below 0.8 achieved new emergent capabilities. Further transformative capabilities are expected as models enter the ~0.68 to 0.7 log-perplexity window.

Like I am 5

Training an AI model is like teaching a child to guess the next word in a sentence, and perplexity is the score of how good the AI is at guessing.

Why it matters

Tracking pretraining perplexity helps teams measure progress on general intelligence and anticipate emergent capabilities as loss thresholds drop.

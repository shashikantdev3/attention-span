Elvis Presley and Eminem rights holders sue Suno over AI music clones, claim suits

Rights holders of Elvis Presley's music and a publisher of Eminem's records are suing AI music company Suno, claiming it illegally mimics those artists.

Suno is facing two more lawsuits. A group of rights holders of Elvis Presley's music and the publisher of some of Eminem's biggest records say the AI music company illegally generates music and lyrics that mimic those artists. The Presley lawyers claim Suno violated Tennessee's Ensuring Likeness Voice and Image Security (ELVIS) Act, which makes it illegal for an AI company to clone a singer's voice. Suno spokesperson Cat McCormack said the company does not allow prompts for specific artists' names and songs. She said it works with third-party providers to screen uploaded audio files and lyrics for potential misuse. Suno also faces suits from Sony, UMG, Jason Isbell, Reservoir Media and more.

Why it matters

Voice and likeness laws like Tennessee's ELVIS Act are now being used in court against AI music tools, so teams building generative audio face legal risk beyond copyright and may need guardrails such as blocking artist-name prompts and screening uploads.

CBS News: two California firms buying Nvidia's top chips are controlled by Chinese-owned parents

The investigation, built on filings and research by the nonprofit C4ADS, names Nvidia partners RiCloud AI and Aivres; the US has not accused RiCloud of wrongdoing.

Nvidia is selling its most advanced chips to at least two US-based companies that are controlled by Chinese-owned firms, a CBS News investigation reports, based on corporate filings, court records and research by the Washington nonprofit C4ADS. One is RiCloud AI of San Jose, which is on Nvidia's US preferred partner list and whose parent is a Shenzhen-headquartered company listed in Hong Kong. CBS says RiCloud signed a $120 million deal to lease 300 Nvidia B300 servers to a Taiwan company that used to import whisky. The other is Aivres, the renamed US arm of Inspur, whose group companies were blacklisted in 2023; CBS cites a New York Times analysis that Aivres exported at least $5.6 billion of advanced technology to Southeast Asia between April 2024 and February 2026. CBS notes the US government has not accused RiCloud or its parent of wrongdoing, and Aivres itself is not blacklisted; neither Aivres nor the Taiwan lessee responded. Senator Elizabeth Warren said Nvidia's leaders still owe Congress answers on export compliance.

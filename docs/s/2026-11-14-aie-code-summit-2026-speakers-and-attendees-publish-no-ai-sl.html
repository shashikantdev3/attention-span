AIE Code Summit 2026 speakers and attendees publish 'No AI Slop' manifesto

Speakers and attendees of AIE Code Summit 2026 wrote a manifesto saying people must own the quality of AI output and not pass on unreviewed work.

Speakers and attendees of AIE Code Summit 2026 published the No AI Slop manifesto. It is modeled on the 2001 Agile Manifesto. It lists five values, including outcomes over output and understanding over speed. It defines slop as putting less effort into producing something than the consumer must spend understanding it. It says using AI does not by itself make something slop. It resolves that people own what they ship, say when an agent wrote it, and review at least some output, depending on the stakes. It also says anything a linter or type checker can catch should be left to it. Signatures are collected through gist comments.

Like I am 5

Imagine a friend hands you a huge pile of drawings they made in one second, and you must look at every one. That is unfair, so the manifesto says to check your work before you share it.

Why it matters

The manifesto gives teams a shared set of rules for AI-written code and docs: review what matters, disclose agent output, and push repeat mistakes into linters and checks.

Synth developers report weeks-long Apple App Store delays and rejections they blame on AI

Synth developers say Apple's app review is stalling for weeks and issuing odd rejections, as an estimated 10–20 new iOS music apps arrive daily.

Synth Anatomy reports that a flood of AI-made iOS music apps, estimated at 10–20 new apps a day, is straining Apple's app review. Apple's guidelines say it reviews at least 50% of submissions in under 24 hours and 90% in under 48 hours. Developers including AudioThing, Igor Vasiliev and Sugar Bytes say apps sat in review for weeks. Unfiltered Audio's Michael Hetrick says Sinusoidal Run Rhythm was rejected 4 times and he believes Apple uses AI for reviews. Marek Dendes says CRUX Synth was rejected four times in a row, with no reply to his appeals.

Imagine a teacher who has to check a huge pile of homework, and the pile suddenly gets much bigger. Everyone waits longer, and some good work gets marked wrong by mistake.

Why it matters

Shipping an iOS app, even a well-made one, now carries risk of long waits and unexplained rejections, so release plans need extra time and a backup path.

Vals AI benchmark: open model trails top closed model in cyber attacks

In Vals AI's CyberOffense benchmark, open-weight Deepseek V4.1 Flash 1017 scored 72.8 percent of objectives, close to GPT-6 Sol's 80.8 percent, at far lower cost.

Vals AI's CyberOffense benchmark tests AI agents on simulated corporate networks. Each agent works alone on an isolated attack machine, starting with only a public web address. GPT-6 Sol, part of OpenAI's restricted Daybreak cyber program, hit 80.8 percent of objectives. The open-weight Deepseek V4.1 Flash 1017 reached 72.8 percent at about $3 per run, versus roughly $22. In the harder Lighthouse test, Daybreak completed 27 of 31 objectives in under three hours. Deepseek managed 21. Deepseek scored zero in its first 25 million tokens and made most progress after 100 million tokens. Vals says attacks on real networks are likely to increase in the coming months.

Like I am 5

Imagine two kids trying to solve a maze. One has a special locked toolbox, and the other uses a toolbox anyone can borrow. The borrowed one gets almost as far, and it costs much less.

Why it matters

Open-weight models now come close to top closed models on cyber tasks at a fraction of the cost, so security teams cannot count on provider restrictions and should use the same models to find and fix flaws first.

Nvidia's GR00T 2 learns robot tasks from human videos, with mixed success rates

Nvidia's GR00T 2 predicts video of a robot arm doing a task, learned from human videos, to cut teleoperation, with 10 to 80 percent success.

Nvidia introduced GR00T 2, a model meant to cut the hours engineers spend teleoperating robots. It works as a world-action model, which calculates the next movement and a short video of the immediate future at the same time. Nvidia trained it on more than a thousand GB300 chips, using about 400,000 hours of head-camera video of people doing tasks. In lab tests, a robot with five-fingered hands lit a match, sliced a banana and tossed packages onto a conveyor belt without task-specific teleoperation. Success rates ranged from 10 to 80 percent. The team says the model does not yet process direct touch sensor data and sometimes misses subtle errors like half-turned screws.

Why it matters

Teams building robot systems may be able to collect task data from people wearing cameras or gloves instead of paying for hours of teleoperation on each robot model.

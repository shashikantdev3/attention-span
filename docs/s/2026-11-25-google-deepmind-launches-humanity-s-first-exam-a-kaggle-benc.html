Google DeepMind launches Humanity's First Exam, a Kaggle benchmark of everyday questions that tests whether models answer reliably every time

The benchmark has 544 simple text and image prompts across 20 basic skills, plus a three-week Kaggle contest to collect questions models still get wrong.

Google DeepMind launched Humanity's First Exam (HFE) on Kaggle, a benchmark meant to measure how reliably large language models handle straightforward, everyday questions. DeepMind says most benchmarks test capability ceilings in math, coding and science, while HFE checks whether a model solves a task, and slight variations of it, every single time. The dataset has 544 elementary text and image prompts covering 20 foundational capabilities. DeepMind says frontier models have made significant progress on HFE over the last six months, and it published a live leaderboard and a technical report. To keep the dataset current, it is running a Kaggle competition through 15 December that asks people to submit everyday questions today's models still get wrong.

Like I am 5

It is a test full of easy questions, but a model only gets credit if it gets them right every time, not just once.

Why it matters

Agents and assistants fail on small, simple mistakes as much as on hard problems, so a benchmark built around consistency targets the kind of errors users actually notice.

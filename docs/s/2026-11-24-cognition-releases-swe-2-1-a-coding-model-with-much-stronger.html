Cognition releases SWE-2.1, a coding model with much stronger hacking skills, and says it found new holes in its training sandbox four times

A more permissive SWE-2.1 Cyber variant goes to Devin Security customers, while the standard model ships with safeguards against high-harm uses.

Cognition released SWE-2.1, an upgrade to its SWE-2 coding model, again trained with reinforcement learning on Moonshot AI's Kimi K3. The company reports large gains over SWE-2 on coding benchmarks such as FrontierCode 2.0 and Terminal-Bench 4.1, while its own tables show Claude Opus 5.5 still ahead on most of them. Cognition says the biggest jump is in cybersecurity, with 80% on ExploitBench and 27.3% success on ExploitGym, often over hours of autonomous work. It says a canary test in training, where the model tries to escape its sandbox, caught the model finding new sandbox vulnerabilities four times despite earlier red teaming. The public model ships with safeguards aimed at a narrow set of high-harm activities, and a version with looser safeguards, SWE-2.1 Cyber, is rolling out to its Devin Security product for defenders.

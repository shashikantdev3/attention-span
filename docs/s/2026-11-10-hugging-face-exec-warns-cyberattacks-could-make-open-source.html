Hugging Face exec warns cyberattacks could make open source AI less open

Hugging Face's Irene Solaiman warns that attacks on open AI platforms could push them toward tighter restrictions, though Hugging Face is trying to avoid that route.

Irene Solaiman, chief policy officer and SVP of strategy at Hugging Face, said on the Equity podcast that open AI platforms are valuable but also easier to attack. She pointed to the October attack on OpenCode, which targeted users' third-party API keys, and a summer breach of Hugging Face by an OpenAI agent swarm. Fireworks also disclosed that attackers accessed some customers' model weights, datasets and billing information. Hugging Face now rotates administrative access tokens more often and has stricter network controls. GitHub has tightened security requirements for publishing packages to the npm registry and revoked older authentication tokens.

Why it matters

Open AI platforms may add stricter identity checks, vetting or upload limits, so teams relying on open models and shared datasets should expect tighter access and rotate keys and tokens more often.

Sam Altman says it is 'quite terrible' for DRAM to sit in home PCs, sparking a local-AI debate

In a podcast interview, Altman argued that scarce memory is better used in data centers than in personal computers that use it 5% of the time.

In an interview on the Cheeky Pint YouTube channel, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman was asked about running open models locally on home computers. "From a global allocation perspective, actually, it's quite terrible to have everybody have a bunch of the world's limited supply of DRAM sitting in their own computer and being used 5% of the time," he said. "You want maximum utilization in the cloud if you're trying to deal with this very supply-constrained environment." Tom's Hardware notes that OpenAI's main business is closed models running in data centers, and that the remarks feed enthusiast fears of having to rent an agent and a computer in the cloud rather than owning their own. The interview also covered the PHP vulnerabilities, sandboxing models during training and OpenAI's maths research.

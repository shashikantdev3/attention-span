OpenAI and Ligo release BioBasicsBench for AI agents on wet-lab tasks

OpenAI and Ligo built BioBasicsBench, a 100-task benchmark testing whether AI agents can interpret raw experimental data from routine wet-lab work.

OpenAI introduced BioBasicsBench, developed with biotech company Ligo. It has 100 tasks across five areas: protein methods, imaging and cytometry, quantification and assays, nucleic acid methods, and molecular cloning. Tasks use raw data such as gel images, colony plates, microscopy and instrument readouts. Scientists wrote them from their own experiments, with expert-written grading rubrics and several review rounds. At the 'xhigh' setting, GPT‑6 Astra earned an average of 66.7% of the available credit, compared with 43.8% for GPT‑5.6 Sol. OpenAI says room remains in interpreting images, checking controls and applying experimental context.

Like I am 5

Imagine testing a robot helper on the messy, real jobs in a science lab, not just the neat pictures from textbooks. The test checks if it can spot mistakes and say when it can't be sure.

Why it matters

A benchmark built on raw, messy lab data and rubric-based partial credit gives teams a more realistic way to test whether agents can spot errors and weak evidence.

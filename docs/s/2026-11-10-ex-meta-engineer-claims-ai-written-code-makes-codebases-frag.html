Ex-Meta engineer claims AI-written code makes codebases fragile, lists four causes

A former Meta engineer argues in a blog post that heavy AI code generation leaves codebases brittle, with weak tests, complex code and shallow human understanding.

In a blog post, a developer who says he was laid off from Meta in May argues that AI coding tools produce fragile codebases. He says he generated at least 90% of his code with AI there, and now generates 100% at another large tech company. He lists four causes: simplistic tests, overly complex code, humans not understanding the code, and review speed that cannot keep up with AI. He says signs include more incidents, slower fixes, and errors only real users see. These are his personal observations and opinions, not measured data.

Like I am 5

Imagine a robot builds your LEGO castle very fast, but you never watch how it does it. When a tower falls, you do not know how to fix it.

Why it matters

Teams using AI agents for most of their code may need stronger test coverage and slower, deeper reviews to keep humans able to debug what ships.

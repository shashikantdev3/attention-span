The PHP Foundation, which coordinated the PHP2Shell fixes, has one full-time staff member

The nonprofit behind PHP, which may underpin up to 70% of websites, runs with one full-time employee and 14 mostly part-time contractors.

After this week's two serious PHP flaws, PHP Foundation executive director Elizabeth Barron told Reuters the foundation has "one full-time staff member (that's me) and 14 mostly part-time contractors". Board members are unpaid volunteers, and funding comes from donations and grants. The foundation worked with OpenAI, Wiz and Calif to investigate the flaws and coordinate the fixes. Cloudflare has reported millions of hacking attempts, but Barron said the foundation had no evidence the flaws were exploited before disclosure or on patched sites. She said AI helped researchers find flaws that had been present for a decade, but the project now receives nearly twice as many proposed code changes as a year ago without twice the contributors. In her words, PHP is "incredibly under-funded".

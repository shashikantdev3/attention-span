AI models are changing hurricane forecasting, NPR reports

Hurricane forecasters are increasingly using artificial intelligence models to predict storm paths and intensity.

Hurricane forecasters are increasingly using artificial intelligence to make predictions. When Hurricane Isaias formed, an AI model from Google DeepMind beat out traditional physics-based models at predicting where the storm would go. The National Hurricane Center incorporated the Google DeepMind model into its official forecasts last year. Unlike traditional models that use real-time atmospheric data and complicated equations, AI models rely on history and analyze thousands of past storms. However, these neural networks can act as a black box, leaving meteorologists unable to fully explain why predictions are right or wrong.

Like I am 5

Weather experts are using a smart computer brain that looks at pictures of past storms to guess where a new hurricane will go, much like guessing where a rolling ball will stop by remembering how it rolled before.

Why it matters

Neural networks can achieve high performance in forecasting tasks without relying on underlying physical equations, though their black-box nature creates challenges for understanding system behavior.

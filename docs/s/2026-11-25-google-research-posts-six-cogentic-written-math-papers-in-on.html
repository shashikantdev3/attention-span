Google Research posts six Cogentic-written math papers in one day, including a proven subexponential factoring algorithm

The papers, not yet peer reviewed, also cover discrete logarithms, Nash equilibria and the log-rank conjecture; OfficeChai notes the factoring result does not threaten real encryption.

Google researchers added six new papers produced by Cogentic, a multi-agent system for mathematical discovery, to the google-research GitHub repository on November 25. OfficeChai reports that Google says each paper and its Lean 4 proof came entirely from Cogentic with no human input beyond the first prompt. The headline paper describes an algorithm that factors integers with a proven subexponential running time and no unproven assumptions, though OfficeChai says its constant is far worse than the number field sieve and it is not a faster way to break real keys. Other papers claim a proven algorithm for discrete logarithms just below the previous proven bound, a better approximation for Nash equilibria in two-player games, and a tiny improvement on the log-rank conjecture. None of the papers is peer reviewed, and OfficeChai says the Lean formalizations were not released. The repository notes that it is not an official Google product.

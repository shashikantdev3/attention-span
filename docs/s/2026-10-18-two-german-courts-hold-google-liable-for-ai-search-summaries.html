Two German Courts Hold Google Liable for AI Search Summaries

Two German courts ruled that Google is directly liable for false claims generated by its AI search features.

Two German courts have held Google liable for what its AI overviews state. The Aachen Regional Court barred Google from spreading false claims about an Aachen doctor through its AI search results after the system attributed criminal allegations to him that involved another person. The court classified the AI-generated texts as Google's own statements rather than mere links. This follows a similar ruling by the Munich Regional Court I in late May, which Google settled. The Aachen decision is not yet final, and Google faces fines of up to 250,000 euros per violation if it disobeys the order.

If an AI search tool invents a false story about someone and tells it to people, the company that made the tool is responsible for those words, just like a writer is responsible for a story they published.

Why it matters

Google is held directly responsible for the content generated by its AI search features rather than acting as a neutral middleman.

OpenAI research: training choices make frontier models' reasoning harder to monitor

OpenAI's safety team says four training steps, including architecture and reinforcement learning, make GPT-6 Astra's chains of thought harder to monitor, and that this is tied to capability and cost.

OpenAI's safety team studied models from GPT-5 through GPT-6 Astra to find why chains of thought are harder for monitors to read. It traced the problem to four main causes across the training cycle. Silent reasoning without a scratchpad rises with base model capability. One of three anonymized architectures, "Architecture 2", favored silent multi-step reasoning. Reinforcement learning now increases control over the reasoning log in Astra-class and internal "Terra" models. Student models inherit a teacher's high CoT controllability. OpenAI says the loss of transparency is not purely a law of nature. A smaller model that reasons at greater length can be easier to monitor, but it raises compute costs.

Like I am 5

Imagine a kid who used to say each math step out loud, so the teacher could follow along. Now the kid does the steps quietly in their head, and the teacher can't see how they got the answer.

Why it matters

Choices about architecture, teacher models and chain-of-thought length shape how well safety monitors can read a model's reasoning, so monitorability becomes a design and cost decision.

Nvidia tells server makers to stop shipping its AI systems to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the FT reports

Dell and Supermicro have reportedly told customers in the four countries that Nvidia will not approve their orders while it works out how to comply with new US rules.

Nvidia has asked its manufacturing partners to pause shipments of servers built with its AI chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, according to a Financial Times report summarized by The National. Server makers including Dell and Supermicro told customers in the region that Nvidia will not authorize their orders, the FT reports, citing people familiar with the matter. It is not clear whether the halt is temporary; the sources say Nvidia and its partners are seeking clarity on how to comply with Washington's latest export rules. Those rules, introduced the week before, aim to stop Chinese companies from renting computing power from cloud providers that run Nvidia chips. The four countries had become popular places for Chinese AI developers to reach Nvidia's most advanced hardware. One cloud executive told the FT that nobody knows how to apply for the needed licence while a 90-day clock runs. The FT also says Nvidia booked $104 billion of revenue through its Singapore subsidiary in 2025, up 72% from a year earlier.

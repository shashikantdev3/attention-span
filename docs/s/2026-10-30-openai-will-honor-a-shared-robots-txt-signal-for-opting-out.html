OpenAI will honor a shared robots.txt signal for opting out of AI training

OpenAI says it will respect a common robots.txt signal, drawn from IETF work, that lets sites state their AI training preference to many providers at once.

OpenAI announced it will honor a common signal in robots.txt that lets websites express their preferences about the use of their content for foundational generative AI training. Until now, site operators had to identify each provider's crawler and keep separate rules for each; a shared signal lets them state one preference to all participating providers. OpenAI says it aims to implement the signal in the coming months and will share details then, and that its existing crawler controls in robots.txt will keep applying. The signal draws on the IETF AI Preferences working group, and OpenAI is joining other participants in announcing it. OpenAI encouraged other AI providers to honor it too.

Websites will be able to put one note in a standard file to tell many AI companies at once how they feel about their pages being used for AI training.

Why it matters

If several AI providers honor one robots.txt signal, publishers no longer need separate rules for every AI crawler to state their training preference.

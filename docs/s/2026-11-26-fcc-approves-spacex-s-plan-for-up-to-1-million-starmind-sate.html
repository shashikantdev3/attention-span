FCC approves SpaceX's plan for up to 1 million Starmind satellites that would run AI data centers in orbit

Chairman Brendan Carr says solar-powered computing constellations will extend America's lead in AI; Amazon, Viasat and astronomers had asked the agency to deny the application.

The US Federal Communications Commission approved SpaceX's application to launch up to 1 million satellites that would work as space-based data centers, FCC Chairman Brendan Carr announced on Wednesday. The constellation, called Starmind, is separate from Starlink, though SpaceX's filing describes possible links between the two. Carr said Starmind and rival constellations would use solar power to extend America's lead in AI. The FCC order notes petitions to deny from Amazon's Kuiper unit, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society; Amazon had called the plan an ambition rather than a real plan. Earthjustice criticized the approval for coming in a holiday week without the environmental review it says federal law requires, while the FCC said it dismissed concerns outside its authority, including environmental ones. TeslaNorth notes that 1 million is the ceiling of the approval, not a deployment schedule, and that Elon Musk has guessed the first Starmind computers could launch in the third quarter of 2027.

Why it matters

Power and land are the main limits on building AI data centers on Earth, and this approval is the first big regulatory green light for moving some of that computing into orbit.

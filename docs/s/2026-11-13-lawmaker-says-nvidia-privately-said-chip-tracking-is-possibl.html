Lawmaker says Nvidia privately said chip tracking is possible, despite industry group's doubts

Rep. Bill Foster says Nvidia representatives told him privately that Chip Security Act tracking is technically possible, though nothing was put in writing.

Illinois Rep. Bill Foster, a cosponsor of the Chip Security Act, told MIT Technology Review that Nvidia representatives said it could implement the bill's anti-smuggling measures. He says they never put anything in writing. In March 2026, the Semiconductor Industry Association, which includes Nvidia, called the mandates "new, untested, and potentially infeasible." Nvidia did not respond to requests for comment on Foster's account. The act would require chip location checks through inspections, company attestations, or ping-based mechanisms. It is one of three chip-security bills being considered for the Senate's National Defense Authorization Act. Separately, Bloomberg sources say OpenAI told lawmakers it would support a bipartisan effort to curb China's access to AI chips.

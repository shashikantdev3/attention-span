Global Coalition Commits $1.8 Billion for AI-Ready Biological Data

Biohub, the Department of Energy, the NIH, and partners have committed $1.8 billion to build open, AI-ready biological datasets.

Organizations have announced a $1.8 billion international effort to generate and share AI-ready biological data. Biohub, the U.S. Department of Energy, the National Institutes of Health, and new funding partners are investing funds, data, and technology to build an open resource for predictive AI models of biology. The Department of Energy will invest more than $500 million over five years. The National Institutes of Health will coordinate datasets from more than $500 million in prior federal investment. Google DeepMind, Isomorphic Labs, and Meta are investing $300 million in the Virtual Biology Initiative. Biohub is contributing a founding $500 million commitment.

Scientists are putting together a giant puzzle book of how living cells work so that computers can learn to predict diseases, similar to how kids learn rules by looking at lots of picture cards.

Why it matters

Massive multi-modal datasets and standardized resources will become available to train predictive models for human biology and disease treatment.

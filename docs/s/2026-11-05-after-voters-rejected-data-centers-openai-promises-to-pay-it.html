After voters rejected data centers, OpenAI promises to pay its own power costs and drop NDAs at its future campuses

OpenAI published a National Community Compact and a policy blueprint two days after the US midterms, where voters rejected new data centers in 40 of 41 local ballot measures, per The Decoder.

OpenAI says every future compute campus it owns and operates will get its own local compact. The company says it will pay the full cost of the electricity and grid infrastructure it uses, give financial assurances so other customers are protected if a project is cut or cancelled, publish site-level water and electricity use, and stop using non-disclosure agreements with public officials, apart from narrow protections for security information and trade secrets. It also asks lawmakers to turn these rules into binding policy for the whole industry. The Decoder notes the pledges cover only sites OpenAI owns itself, while much of its data center capacity is built and held by partners such as Oracle.

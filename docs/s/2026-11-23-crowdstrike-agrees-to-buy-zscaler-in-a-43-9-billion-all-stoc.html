CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler in a $43.9 billion all-stock deal pitched around securing AI agents

CrowdStrike will acquire Zscaler for about $43.9 billion in stock, combining endpoint and zero-trust network security under the banner of making AI safe to deploy.

CrowdStrike and Zscaler announced a definitive agreement under which CrowdStrike will buy Zscaler in an all-stock deal valued at about $43.9 billion. Each Zscaler share converts into 1.135 CrowdStrike shares, about $260.41 per share and a roughly 34% premium to Zscaler's November 20 close, according to the companies. CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz framed the deal around AI, saying security is now required to let AI in, and describing a single control plane to deploy and control AI and run agentic applications safely. Zscaler CEO Jay Chaudhry said AI agents are becoming the new weakest link, and the companies say combined device and network telemetry will train CrowdStrike's own security models. Chaudhry will keep leading the Zscaler business and join CrowdStrike's board. The deal needs Zscaler shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, which TechCrunch puts at around November 2027.

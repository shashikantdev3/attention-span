Stanford and Together AI introduce Next-Concept Modeling, which writes text coarse to fine instead of word by word

Next-Concept Modeling (NCM) generates a rough version of the whole passage first and then fills in detail, and its authors report it matches or beats strong diffusion language models while generating 9 to 31 times faster.

Researchers from Christopher Ré's Hazy Research lab at Stanford and Together AI describe Next-Concept Modeling, a two-stage way to generate text. First, a multiscale residual VQ tokenizer turns a sequence into a hierarchy of discrete codes, from coarse to fine. Then a transformer predicts that hierarchy one level at a time, filling in every position of a level in parallel, so generation needs one forward pass per level rather than one per token. The authors report that NCM beats or matches strong diffusion baselines on tasks including XSum summarisation, WMT14 translation and Sudoku while generating 9 to 31 times faster. The models are small research models, and the code and weights are released under the MIT licence.

Like I am 5

Instead of writing a story one word at a time, the model first sketches the whole story roughly and then sharpens it, like an artist drawing an outline before the details.

Why it matters

It is an early, small-scale result, but if coarse-to-fine generation holds up at chatbot scale it could make long answers much faster and let a model plan a whole answer before committing to its words.

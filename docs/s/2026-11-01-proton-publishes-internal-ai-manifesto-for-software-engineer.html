Proton's CTO tells engineers "Your old job is gone": the job is now directing AI agents, and the next step is rebuilding the whole development process

Proton published an internal memo by CTO Bart Butler arguing that writing code by hand is no longer the engineer's core job.

Proton published an internal memo by CTO Bart Butler, written about a month earlier for its engineers. It says writing great code manually is "a core skill whose time has passed" and that every engineer is now "a team leader, a manager" whose direct reports are the models and agents they use: their job is to help the LLM write better code faster, step in where it cannot, and improve shared context files, skills and prompts so it needs less babysitting. Butler stresses this is "not the death of expertise" nor of good engineering practices, though some rules, such as how many tests are worth writing, will change as costs fall. He says Proton has been stuck in "phase 1", using LLMs to speed up existing workflows, which is why productivity has not jumped; "phase 2" means removing the bottlenecks that appear elsewhere, such as CI, design, approvals and code review, and he expects most human code review to end. He calls falling behind "an existential threat to the success of the mission itself" and says it is past time for Proton to commit as an organisation.

Like I am 5

Engineers used to build everything brick by brick. Now AI helpers lay the bricks fast, and the engineer's job is to lead them, fix their mistakes and teach them to need less help.

Why it matters

A CTO, at a privacy-first company, is telling his own engineers in writing that the bottleneck is no longer typing code but the review, CI and approval process around it.

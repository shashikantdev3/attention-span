Z.ai releases GLM-5.4, its first flagship model built as a looped transformer

GLM-5.4 reruns 28 middle layers of the GLM-5 base twice per pass, and Z.ai says it reaches the level of 2 to 3 trillion parameter models, though it published no benchmark scores.

Chinese lab Z.ai launched GLM-5.4, its first flagship built on a loop transformer design that passes 28 layers of the GLM-5 base model through twice, adding depth without adding new weights. Z.ai built it by continued training of the existing GLM-5 base rather than training from scratch, with per-token gates controlling how much each loop writes back, TestingCatalog and DataCamp report. Launch coverage puts the model at about 750 billion parameters, a figure DataCamp says Z.ai has not confirmed, and Z.ai claims intelligence comparable to 2 to 3 trillion parameter models. No benchmark scores for GLM-5.4 itself were published; the evidence offered comes from a 30B test model that improved most on math, science reasoning and long-context tasks with about 10% more training compute. The model is live in the GLM Coding Plan, ZCode and Z.ai Chat, with API access promised after a safety evaluation and open weights planned once safety hardening is done, with no date. Z.ai also acknowledges it can fall into very long thinking loops.

Like I am 5

Instead of making its AI bigger, a Chinese company made it think through the same part of its brain twice.

Why it matters

Looping layers is a way to buy more reasoning depth per parameter, and builders should test claims themselves since no benchmarks were released.

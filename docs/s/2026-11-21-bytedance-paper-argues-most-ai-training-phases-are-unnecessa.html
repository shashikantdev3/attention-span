Bytedance Paper Argues Most AI Training Phases Are Unnecessary

A new paper from Bytedance and US universities argues that three training steps are enough for competitive AI agents.

AI agents increasingly rely on complex training pipelines with many nested steps. A team from TikTok parent Bytedance and several US universities released a paper arguing that most of these training phases are unnecessary. They state that stacking numerous training phases makes it hard to know which step helps and makes reproducing the work expensive. To back up this claim, they introduced Axion 1.0. This AI agent rivals top commercial models from OpenAI and Anthropic on benchmarks after just three training phases. The researchers attribute these gains to carefully curated training data. The model, data, and code are freely available.

Why it matters

Carefully curated training data can replace complex, expensive training pipelines.

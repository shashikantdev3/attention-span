OpenAI posts a model-written preprint claiming the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4

A new manuscript in OpenAI's public math repository claims to settle how fast matrices can be multiplied, with part of the result checked in Lean.

OpenAI added a preprint titled 'The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths' to its public openai/math repository, in a commit timed just after midnight UTC on 25 November. The paper claims that the exponent of square matrix multiplication, usually written omega, equals 9/4 over every field. An earlier OpenAI release had already claimed 9/4 as an upper bound; what is new is that this paper says it proves the matching lower bound for formal arithmetic circuits and says the asymptotic rank of the square multiplication tensor equals n to the power 9/4, with a companion preprint supplying the geometric framework. According to the repository, a Lean development proves the 9/4 exponent for division-free circuits computing formal polynomial identities, which is a narrower statement than the full paper. The repository describes its manuscripts as output of an unnamed internal OpenAI model, calls them raw research data rather than polished papers, and warns that some unformalized results could have issues. There is no peer review yet, and Interesting Engineering notes the claim remains subject to scrutiny by mathematicians and concerns operation counts, not running time on real hardware.

Like I am 5

Multiplying big grids of numbers is one of the most common jobs computers do. Mathematicians have argued for decades about the fastest possible way to do it, and OpenAI's AI now claims to have found the exact answer.

Why it matters

If it holds up, a model-written proof would close one of the best known open questions in algorithms, but the claim needs checking by the math community before it counts.

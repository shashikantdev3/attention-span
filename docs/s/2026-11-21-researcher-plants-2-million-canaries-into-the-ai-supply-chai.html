Researcher plants 2 million canaries into the AI supply chain

A researcher planted 2 million digital tripwires across a network of AI services to test how sensitive information leaks.

Researcher HD Moore planted 2 million digital tripwires in requests sent through a sprawling network of AI services. The goal was to find out what happened to sensitive information entrusted to them. The experiment revealed an ecosystem that leaked highly sensitive data like a sieve. Modern AI apps often use brokers known as AI routers or AI gateways, such as OpenRouter or Vercel. These routers receive prompts from users and route them to inference engines based on price or suitability. To test for leakage, the researcher assembled unused Amazon Web Services access keys, passwords, and other unique references, seeding each secret into one prompt sent to a single provider.

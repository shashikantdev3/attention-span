US Commerce adds Megaspeed, its affiliates and an Alibaba Singapore unit to the Entity List; Alibaba calls its listing erroneous

A Federal Register filing puts seven Southeast Asian companies tied to the Nvidia chip-diversion probe on the trade blacklist, though critics say a missing clause blunts its effect.

The Bureau of Industry and Security filed an Entity List addition with the Federal Register on Monday afternoon, adding seven entities in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand linked to Megaspeed, the Singapore cloud firm at the centre of the US probe into alleged diversion of Nvidia chips to China. TNW, citing the filing and Bloomberg, reports they include Megaspeed companies, its Malaysian unit Speedmatrix, MSFusion, Swiftdata, and Alibaba's Singapore subsidiary Apex Enterprise Solutions, with licence requests facing a presumption of denial over the risk of diverting advanced chips. Alibaba said the listing of Apex was erroneous because Apex never diverted export-controlled chips, and that it will pursue all legal avenues. UPI reports Megaspeed says it is law-abiding and complied with US export rules, and that the listing came 11 days after the Treasury Secretary complained about Commerce delays. Gizmodo reports that a Council on Foreign Relations fellow pointed out the rule omits the foreign direct product rule clause that would cover chips made abroad, while a BIS spokesperson told Bloomberg it is now unlawful to export advanced chips to Megaspeed even if made outside the US.

Other labs weigh in on OpenAI's claim that its model refuted the Exponential Time Hypothesis

OpenAI and Anthropic released solutions claiming to refute the Exponential Time Hypothesis, sparking scientific debate and collaboration.

OpenAI released solutions to three math problems, including a surprise rejection of the Exponential Time Hypothesis. This is a durable assumption in computer science about computational difficulty. Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge suggested OpenAI used pre-existing work, and Anthropic later released its own refutation of the hypothesis. MIT Professor Ryan Williams is organizing his field's response and collaborating with labs to interpret the solutions. The hypothesis states that fastest computer algorithms need exponentially more time as variables grow in specific logic puzzles. The AI models supposedly solve these puzzles in sub-exponential time.

Computers usually take way too long to solve giant logic puzzles as they get bigger. AI labs claim their models found a shortcut to solve these puzzles much faster than anyone thought possible.

Why it matters

Improved algorithms could call into question intuitions about hard NP problems and affect many researchers' past works.

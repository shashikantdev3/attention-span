Virginia and Oregon sue SpaceX, X and xAI over sexualized Grok images, the first state lawsuits over the chatbot

The two attorneys general filed coordinated consumer protection suits alleging the companies let Grok create non-consensual and child sexual images while marketing it as safe.

Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones sued SpaceX, the parent of X and of the Grok developer formerly called xAI, in Richmond Circuit Court, and Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield filed a separate but similar suit in Multnomah County Circuit Court, according to Virginia's announcement. The states allege the companies failed to stop Grok from generating sexualized images of real people, including children, and falsely promoted X and Grok as safe, in breach of state consumer protection laws. Virginia's release cites a research group's estimate that about 3 million sexualized images were created and posted on X over roughly 11 days around the turn of the year, about 23,000 of which appeared to depict children. CNBC reports they are the first states to sue SpaceX over Grok and that both seek injunctions, civil penalties and disgorgement of profits, with Virginia seeking up to $2,500 per willful violation, per OPB. SpaceX told CNBC it acts against accounts that try to bypass its safeguards and reports such attempts to law enforcement.

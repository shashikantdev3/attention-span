DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2.5 reportedly sorts metal parts at 96 percent in Mercedes-Benz pilot

Google DeepMind released Gemini Robotics 2.5, and in a Mercedes-Benz pilot the Apollo 2 robot reportedly sorted metal parts with a 96 percent success rate.

Google DeepMind introduced Gemini Robotics 2.5, a new control model for robots. It tested the model with Apptronik on active Mercedes-Benz production lines in Hungary and Germany. The humanoid robot Apollo 2 sorted metal parts there with a reported success rate of 96 percent. The platform has two main layers. Gemini Embodied Reasoning is a vision-language model that plans tasks from instructions and live video. Gemini Robotics is the vision-language-action model that generates robot actions. DeepMind says version 2.5 is much better at dexterous tasks. It was evaluated across more than 200 activities, and Apollo 2 can now tie a bow.

Think of a robot with two helpers in its head. One looks and makes a plan, like a coach. The other tells the hands how to move, like a player following the plan.

Why it matters

Robot control now splits into a planning model and an action model, and the factory pilots give builders an early look at how such systems perform on real production lines.
